Attributes analysis

Archon Shiva — 10/25/25, 11:04 PM
Let's do a bit of looking at the value of raising attributes.

First of all, let's define a few terms I will use in this context:  *Offensive* makes you useful to the party, lets you accomplish goals; *Defensive* prevents bad things from happening to you, and *Utility* is something else on the side, usually that improves what's already there. Lockpicking is *Offensive* in this context.

Generally, the party wants to be a walking blob of *Offensive* abilities, including killing monsters, finding loot, etc.  *Defensive* stuff is a cp tax.

__DEXTERITY__
**DX** [20] (Includes Speed [5] and HMD [5])
You get almost the entire offensive utility of DX from one weapon skill at [4] per level, uncapped.  Basic Speed [5] is pretty good Utility, but that leaves a gap of [11] per level of DX you're never getting back. As a result, it's very hard to justify increasing DX in this campaign.

__INTELLIGENCE__
**Per** [5] (includes Acute X [2])
In the medium and long term, Per is a prime *Defensive* attribute, as it lets you avoid being surprised and losing a turn.  It is the second most rolled thing after your weapon skill, or *before* if you're not a fighter.
**Will** [5]
Mainly *Defensive*, and the easiest attribute to *replace*, as many features offer "almost Will" for cheaper. It becomes much, much better if you can leverage it offensively (e.g. Thronebreaker).
**IQ** [20] (includes Per[5] and Will[5])
The main Defensive aspect not in Per or Will is hard covered (+6) by Combat Reflexes [15], which is the way to go for fighters.
There are a few Offensive and utility uses, but hard to leverage if you're not a caster.
Wizards, on then other hand, are fully a no-brainer — Wizardly magic covers all bases, and beyond possibly one spell that just keeps giving at crazy levels, and perhaps [2] in the VH ones, IQ 16 is way cheaper than increasing a bunch of spells, and includes Per and Will for free.  That gives base skill 16+6-2=20, after which decisions should be made how to handle growth (as 20+6-2 will not hit 25).
Clerics without Magery are aiming for IQ+PI of 17.

__STRENGTH__
**ST** [10] (Includes HP [2], Lifting [3], Striking [5])
Because you can't raise them very far individually (2 for Striking, ~3 for the others), you're basically raising ST as a way to access higher Striking ST.
**HP** [2]
Defensive.  I'm a strong believer in not taking damage, but having your HP go up as a side effect of raising ST is neat.
**Lifting ST** [3]
Utility.  There are often cheaper ways to carry more stuff, but if raising ST is going to happen eventually, it's a very good "now" shortcut.
**Striking ST** [5]
Offensive, and DFRPG *seriously* rewards high damage numbers.  Generally, if you hit things fr damage, you want to target 17 for swing 3d-1 to get +6 from Weapon Master, so assume ST 15 +2 Striking ST.  If you have access to an item that gives +1, then ST 18+2+1 hits 4d-1.  A Strength Potion with Bless should reliably give you another die.

__HEALTH__
**Hard to Kill** [1]
Note that in DFRPG you start at 10(+2), because Mortal Wounds are highly survivable.  If you need a death check, you probably already used Luck and lost your Bless, so try to get this to at least 12(+2) by the time you need to roll it.  It's cheap.
**Speed** [5]
Sequence is flat order.  There is no gain to being 0.25 behind someone rather than 1.75 behind them.  Monsters, which do not put [32] into weapon skills, have such high basic speeds compared to us that it makes little sense for us to compete — ETS might be cheaper.
It increases Dodge, though, if you're really focusing on that and have all the cheap tools (Defensive).
**FP** [3].  
If you're not Trained by a Master, you don't need this.
**HT** [10] (Includes Speed [5], FP [3], Hard to kill [1])
Defensive, and remaining conscious is really cool, but if you're not a caster, Fit [5] should be your first step.
A few gems hidden here — Some Utility (Singing, Carousing, Sex Appeal) and one serious Offensive use: Kiai.  If you can leverage these, it makes raising HT *really* interesting.
