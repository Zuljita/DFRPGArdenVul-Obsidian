Archon Shiva — 3/9/25, 2:17 PM
When can Blink Other be used?
Can it target the attacker?
Does it become resisted?

dripton — 3/9/25, 2:32 PM
You can use Blocking spells as Regular spells if you want.  (If you want to use Blink to teleport 3 yards, you don't have to ask your friend to punch you so you have an attack to evade.)  Casting time becomes 1s.  You can Blink Other an enemy, but they can resist (see Apportation).  Blink Other against the attacker is not a valid way to block an attack; once they make the attack roll the attack is already successful even if you blink the attacker away.  (Hitting the archer after the arrow leaves the bow doesn't stop the arrow.)  Instead you want to Blink Other the target (normal use of the spell as written) or use a Wait to Blink Other the attacker after they move into attack position but before they roll the attack.  (If they don't have the movement left to get back into attack range this turn, this might cost them  their attack -- but you have to make the decision before seeing their roll rather than getting to wait until you know they hit.)

dripton — 3/9/25, 2:32 PM
Blink Other against a large SM creature costs more, as for Regular spells.

dripton — 3/9/25, 2:36 PM
You can't Blink to a spot you can't see, so it's not useful for blindly bypassing locked doors.  (You *can* use it to bypass a locked door that has a small window in it so you can see where you're going, or if you have a magical way to see through a door, as long as the door doesn't have magical anti-teleport protection.)  You can't Blink Other an enemy into a wall, but if you're near hazardous terrain you can abuse it to put them in an open place where gravity is not their friend.

dripton — 3/9/25, 2:38 PM
Outdoors or in a room with high ceilings, you can Blink Other an enemy 3 yards straight up for minor falling damage.

dripton — 3/9/25, 2:44 PM

dripton — 3/9/25, 2:54 PM
It does not detect friend or enemy; you just pick a target.  Similarly the target can always choose whether or not to resist, whether they're your friend or not.  You only get to use the Blocking spell rules in one particular case: right after they roll the attack, as a defense roll that actually has a chance of defending.  Since Blink Other on the attacker doesn't defend, you don't get to do it as a Blocking spell.  You instead have to do it as a Regular spell, which means it's a Concentrate maneuver and takes your turn.  But you can use a Wait.

dripton — 3/9/25, 2:56 PM
Basically, the only time you can use a Blocking spell to violate the turn order rules is when you're *actually* using it as an Active Defense, not when you're abusing it.  Abuse it on your own time.

A pre-answer before someone asks:

dripton — 3/16/25, 9:09 PM
"If a mercenary takes Very Wealthy, how can the PCs use this?"

dripton — 3/16/25, 9:13 PM
1. PCs only get loot XP for the loot they earned and kept.  If a Very Wealthy mercenary helped them sell their loot for more but then kept a lot of the gain for themself as a fee, the PC's only get loot XP for the part of the loot they actually got.  Example: item worth $1000, PC with average wealth could sell for $400, Very Wealthy merc says they'll give them $500 for it, PC gets $500 and XP based on $500 (more than before!), Very Wealthy merc gets Very Wealthier.

dripton — 3/16/25, 9:13 PM
2. You can only use Very Wealthy for loot XP if that mercenary was in play on that adventure.  You can't have him offscreen in the background generating extra XP.

dripton — 3/16/25, 9:14 PM
3. You *can* totally keep stuff unsold until you have the Very Wealthy merc around to sell it, to make you more money, but by then it's too late to get loot XP for it.  So you do get value for a Very Wealthy part-time mercenary PC, but not nearly as much as you'd get from a Very Wealthy full-time PC.
